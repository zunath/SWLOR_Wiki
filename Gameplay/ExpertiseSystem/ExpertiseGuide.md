# What are Expertise and Mastery?
Expertise represents a character’s training, talent, and/or general aptitude with a particular skill or ability. Having Expertise in something by definition means your character is above and beyond the average person, the standard user, the rank and file, when it comes to the field in question. While any Player Character can be considered to be better than the average joe in the galaxy at just about anything (within reason), this is generally just the “notable character” effect — merely by being a Player Character, an individual worth having a story told, you are as competent or better than a normal NPC. With Expertise, you are taking that to the next level, developing your character’s actual focuses and specific practiced skills.

Mastery represents the state of having taken an Expertise to its peak. One Expertise may be designated to be ascended to Mastery level as your character’s capstone, being the skill they have worked on and developed above all others. This is a central, and in part defining aspect of your character. A character with Mastery in something is without question among the greatest in the entire galaxy at that thing, beaten out only by canon plot NPCs of the era known for that ability (i.e., someone with Mastery in Force Drain is still not as powerful with it as Darth Nihilus, and someone with Mastery in Battle Meditation is not as good at it as Bastila Shan).

While we have a list of established Expertises, inventing a new Expertise is also possible so long as it makes sense and Staff deems it suitable and fitting with the setting.

## What *can’t* be an Expertise?
Generally, an Expertise must be something that is a measurable, worthwhile, and actively usable skill, ability, or knowledge, and should be something that makes sense on a scaling system of increasing ability — therefore, anything that does not fit these criteria is unfit to be an Expertise. Some things simply work better as Feats (see “What are Feats?” below) because they are more of a binary, or are simply cleaner to implement in that way.

Any proficiency at purely social or intellectual pursuits, such as Philosophy, are not deemed suitable for Expertises. Things like these rely too much on the player’s ability to roleplay them. Similarly, anything that cannot be reasonably and somewhat clearly defined, anything that cannot be trained and practiced, and anything that provides no actual, discernible benefit should not be proposed as Expertises.

# How does this system work?
This system works on a 3-point scale, with each point representing an additional degree of training and practice towards developing your abilities to greater heights. The terms ‘rank’ and ‘point’ may be used interchangeably here.

- Each Expertise can have a maximum of **three (3)** ranks allocated towards it. 
- A single character can have a maximum of **fifteen (15)** Expertise ranks in total.
	- If you want to hard-focus a narrow set of abilities, this allows for 5 maxed-out Expertises all at 3.
		- We also encourage using this extra wiggle-room to let your characters breathe more with somewhat more spread-out points. 
- **One (1)** Expertise can be chosen per character to become their capstone, or ***Mastery*** - the single ability they have developed the most. This is visually represented by a star symbol (**☆**) beside its rank number (ex: **3☆**), and does not count as an additional point towards your character’s total cap.
	- The ‘Mastery’ represents the same narrative and roleplay function as having a Mastery at rank 5 in the previous system.
	- This may not necessarily have a ‘mechanical’ effect in Dice systems, though a GM is free to give it one; it is more meant to allow a character to attempt more significant actions with their Mastery.
- Each rank of Expertise is generally expected to equate to a bonus towards relevant dice rolls in events and various RP Dice systems - as before, this will vary between systems and depending on how the Staff Member running an event wants things scaled.
	- For example, for a standard d20 system, Expertises can be directly translated to +1/+2/+3. If a Staff Member wants them to be more impactful, they might increase the scaling (ex. +1/+3/+5, +2/+4/+6, so on). In a d100 system, they might be +10/+20/+30.
	- Some Expertises may potentially be able to stack together for bonuses, pending GM approval. For example, someone with Gunslinging and Pistols each at 2 ranks might be able to get a +4 to a roll for a quick-draw shot.
	- It is expected that there will be a period of growing pains as the system is experimented with in-game - but this should ideally be a minor issue.
- Each character also will have the opportunity to apply for a number of ***Feats***, an entirely new feature to this system that will be explained further ahead. A character may have a maximum of **eight (8)** Feats in addition to their 15 Expertise ranks. 
- Some Expertises and Feats are **Rare** or **Mentored**, which means they have some restrictions and requirements. This will be covered later. 
# What are Feats?
Feats, inspired by the same-named system in tabletop systems like Dungeons & Dragons, Pathfinder, and other d20-based systems, are ways to represent additional facets and capabilities your character has, without necessarily being things that make sense to be scaling levels of competency. The benefits Feats give are not standardized, and are subject to the rulings of whatever GM may be present and running any given event or interaction, but GMs are strongly encouraged to give them some kind of effect should they become relevant.

More so than Expertises, Feats are meant to be somewhat free-form. There will likely never be a full comprehensive list of feats, but there are a number that we have defined. Like with Expertises, new Feats can be proposed and applied for.

Feats can be things like:
- “**Fluff**” qualities; things that are unlikely to give benefits in most situations, but add to your character. They may still be worth small bonuses or opportunities if they somehow come up, should a GM allow it (this is encouraged). These are encouraged to be things like small quirks, hobbies, simple or non-heroic life skills, mundane professions, and other “background” details.
	- **Examples:** Painter, Dancer, Accountant, Anthropologist, Double-Jointed, Gamer, Gambler
- Specific powers, skills, or abilities that don’t fit into a scaling Expertise format, but are noteworthy and deserving of notation.
	- **Examples:** Shatterpoint {Rare} (Sense 3), Languages (i.e. Tusken Sign, ur-Kittât), EVA Operation
- **Specializations** or **Sub-Feats** of particular Expertises to grant the character additional capabilities or specialized knowledge within a field. Specializations are often chosen for free at the time of taking your first rank in an expertise, and Sub-Feats usually require a minimum rank in their related expertise before being taken.
	- **Examples:** Shipwright (Technician 1), Surgeon (Medical 2), Malacia (Force Healing 2)

Feats are applied for and approved just like Masteries and Expertises — generally “Fluff” feats (see above) do not require approval. 

Each player character starts with two (2) feats representing their journey through life up until the point of arriving in the game world, and it is recommended and expected that these beginning feats will fit the “Fluff” category mentioned above. Feat slots are unlocked at a rate of one for every 3 ranks of Expertise and once a character gains their Mastery (**3☆**), totaling to eight (8) at maximum. GMs may also rarely grant PC’s feats as rewards for events or plotlines, and bonus feats granted in this way do not count towards this cap — though this will be discussed amongst Staff first, and consideration will be taken for any existing GM-granted feats.
# Mentored and Rare Expertises and Feats
While many can be ‘self-trained’ so to speak, or developed and practiced without specific guidance after some initial learning period, some Feats and Expertises have additional restrictions or requirements to be taken. This can be broken down into two categories, which can overlap.

## Mentored
A “Mentored” feat or expertise is one that must be taught to a character by another character who already knows it. This is generally specialized knowledge or abilities that require a particular mindset or method, and cannot be easily accessed through commonly available avenues. While it is expected that some degree of training and learning is happening for all characters in-game (especially when it comes to Force abilities), many of them can be developed further after initial learning without the need for specific extra teaching. Mentored feats and expertises, by comparison, require a PC (or, if needed, NPC) with the feat or ranks in the expertise to act as a teacher.

A Technician can study Engineering and practice their craft on their own time, but to learn the vaunted art of Sith Sorcery one would need a tutor to teach them its dark secrets.

## Rare
A Rare feat or expertise is one that is, as the name suggests, rare: it is very uncommon, a skill or ability only a scarce few in the galaxy possess, whether because it is a heavily guarded secret or because only certain people have what it takes to practice it. An ability that is Rare, but not Mentored, will have a higher degree of scrutiny for approval, and any additional Rares beyond the first will raise the bar further; however, after its initial learning period, it can be advanced through regular use and practice. A Rare and Mentored ability is not only uncommon, but also requires careful teaching by an existing mentor.

Teräs Käsi, the martial art of the Steel Hand, is a Rare and Mentored expertise - very few people in the galaxy know these techniques, and to learn them requires specific guidance and teaching. Instinctive Astrogation, however, is only Rare because it is not something that can be simply taught, and it is not common among Force Sensitives.
# Application, Approval, & Training Time
## Application & Approval
Expertises are applied for via Discord tickets to be reviewed and approved by staff. You can ask for the relevant application form on Discord. To be eligible to apply for Expertise, your character must have at least 200 SP (Skill Points) in-game. You are also required to have the maximum of 50 points in the relevant skill if your requested expertise is related to any of the following mechanical skills:
- Armor
- Any Weapon skill (except Martial Arts)
- Force
- Any Crafting Skill
- Piloting
- First Aid

For anything else not relating to those categories, this requirement is waived. 

Generally speaking, approval for Expertise ranks will require sufficient in-game roleplay build-up demonstrating consistent work towards learning, practicing, and improving at the skill in question. This could be taking lessons from other PCs, using the skill to good effect in events, taking opportunities granted by GMs, spending time in-game practicing and developing your skills (it is strongly encouraged that this be done in the presence of other players and/or GMs; ‘solo’ RP is fine to do, but will carry very little weight in an application), and even using the skill in PvP scenes.

The first rank of any given expertise will require a moderate amount of investment. Generally, staff will want to see evidence of learning RP in particular (especially for Mentored and Rare expertises), and multiple scenes in which your character is training, practicing, or actively using the ability in question. One or two scenes is not enough — shoot for at least four or five examples of your character working towards this, whether they’re succeeding or failing during those examples.

Each subsequent rank after the first raises the bar further. This means either more given examples of using and improving their skills, or greater examples. The higher rank you are aiming for, the more you will need to showcase its use in Staff-overseen scenarios like events and plotlines, and the more effort you will have to put into making these examples demonstrative of higher levels of skill. When applying for Mastery in something (**3☆**), you must be able to present very significant and consistent evidence of your character pushing their skills, knowledge, and capabilities with the Expertise to the limit, with bold and highly effective use in GM-run scenarios, and likely taking risks in the process.

Applying for Feats carries the same general process, but given that they can vary widely in impact, there is a similar variance in how much build-up roleplay will be expected. As a general rule, Mentored and Rare Feats will have a greater expectation than Feats without those tags. Overall however, Feats should take about as much work as Rank 1 in an Expertise as a baseline.

When a character is approved for their first Expertise, an Expertise Sheet will be created and the player will be provided with a screenshot of the sheet to keep on hand for reference until the next time it is updated.

## Training Time
Once approved, an Expertise rank is immediately granted and usable, but there is a period of cooldown time afterwards meant to represent the time spent training and refining your skills before another rank can be granted. 

Up to 3 Expertise ranks can be approved at one time. In cases like this, they are “queued” and become usable sequentially. The first one is available to use immediately, the second becomes available after the cooldown time from the first, and the third becomes available after the cooldown time from the second. 

The very first Expertise rank a character takes has a cooldown of **2 Weeks (14 Days)**; the second, whether for a new Expertise or advancing the first by another rank, has a cooldown of **3 Weeks (21 Days)**. Every subsequent Expertise rank that character gains will have cooldowns of **4 Weeks (28 Days)**.

When Mastery in an Expertise is approved, it has an extended cooldown of **3 Months (84 Days)**. 

Given that their slots are earned at a staggered rate, Feats have no standard cooldown time.

## Retraining
Sometimes, if roleplay develops in certain ways and calls for it, you may want to change out one expertise rank for another — especially if your character is already at their maximum total expertise ranks. In cases like these, you can ***Retrain*** out of one expertise into another, one rank at a time per expertise. To do this, you would apply for the new expertise rank as normal, provide evidence or explanation of the RP put towards it, and make clear that you are requesting to Retrain an existing rank for the new rank. It’s also encouraged that you show this shift of focus in RP, one skill suffering so another can grow. It’s unlikely for a character to simply forget knowledge, but they may fall out of practice, get rusty, or just not be up-to-date on the latest developments in the field, depending what’s appropriate.

Once approved, the swap will be made and the cooldown time will be shortened by half (from 28 days to 14 days).

You can also choose to Retrain Feats, particularly in cases where you may no longer have the required Expertise rank for a particular Feat. The process for this is the same, but with no shortening of cooldown since there is no cooldown to begin with on Feats.
# Retirement
Characters die, or sometimes we simply feel they’ve run their course and no longer wish to play them. When this happens on SWLOR, staff offers the chance to apply for Retirement benefits. What this means is that the original character being retired (if they aren’t dead from IC occurrences) is no longer playable, but a new character receives some starting boost in exchange.

This process is applied for via Discord tickets similarly to requesting Expertise, Mastery, or Feats. You can ask for the relevant application form on Discord. Applying for Retirement of a character that has not died in an Event requires that the character be at least ninety (90) days old from time of creation and have the maximum of 350 skill points in-game.

There are three available Retirement packages. All of them grant a baseline of 100,000 credits, 1 Feat, and 3 Expertise ranks with a cool-down of only 1 Week (7 Days) each, which must all be spread out to different Expertises. If your chosen Feat is an existing one with a requirement, you must meet that requirement with your granted Expertise ranks. Each package has different additional benefits:
1. **Expertise:** One additional Feat and Two (2) additional Expertise ranks granted and usable instantly. These ranks may be used to increase two of your 1-Week cooldown ranks to 2. 
2. **Item:** A special, custom item (counts against the limit of 2, if equipable; equivalent of Tier 5.5), as well as 150,000 extra starting credits and an EXP bonus modifier of 25%.
3. **Ship Crew:** A standard, unmodified Corvette ship to use in-game. This option requires that you have a group in mind and ready to play with to act as your Crew.


# Transfers & Updates From Previous System
Because this system is quite different from the previous Mastery system, existing characters will need to be updated and transferred to the new system. This will provide some challenges, and the process will have some rough parts that cannot be completely smoothed over — but we will do our best to make it as easy as possible for both players and staff.

The previous system worked on a 5-point scale of Mastery ranks. This change to a 3 and 3☆ system makes each individual rank more meaningful and valuable than before, where the difference between each rank was a bit smaller and harder to quantify and, in many systems, the point total would be cut in half and rounded up for dice roll bonuses anyway. Part of the intent of the new system is to remove the need for that extra mental math going downward (dividing and rounding), which can feel unsatisfying, in favor of a baseline that feels agreeable to those GMs who currently use the halve-and-round-up systems and allows for easy scaling up for those who want more heroic or impactful uses of Expertise (see “How does this system work?” above for examples of the intended dice system usage).

Because there’s a bigger gap between ranks of Expertise than the old Mastery ranks, the expectations for applications are generally higher, with a greater burden of support and proof. 

Going forward, existing Mastery ranks will be converted to Expertise ranks thusly:

- Mastery 1-2 → 1 Expertise
- Mastery 3 → 2 Expertise
- Mastery 4 → 3 Expertise
- Mastery 5 → 3☆ Expertise (Mastery)

This presents a slight issue for players who currently have 2 Mastery ranks in any given field, and may feel as if progress or time is being lost. Additionally, some things that were valid Masteries previously are no longer considered such, so some characters may be losing whole lines of training. To mitigate this, we have a few measures in place.

1. Those with either a standing or queued Mastery 2 (which is converted down to 1 rank of Expertise) will be given a ‘Time Token’ which they can use in one of two ways.
**a**. Apply a single-use Time Reduction to the next Expertise rank they’re approved for, cutting the cooldown to a single week - application for this can be done immediately if the player has sufficient roleplay evidence for the rank they’re asking for.
**b**. Immediately shift a queued point over to a new Expertise instead, with the time remaining preserved.
2. Those who have a Mastery 2 and are queued for Mastery 3 will be simply converted to Expertise 2 with the remaining queue time preserved. As the queue time is now a cooldown instead of a cook time, the new Expertise 2 is immediately usable. No token will be given in this case as Expertise 2 is considered equivalent to Mastery 3.
3. If you have an existing old-system Mastery that is no longer valid as an Expertise in the new system (such as Jar’Kai for Lightsabers, or Shatterpoint which is now a Feat), a few options are available.
**a**. You may work with Staff to figure out the closest applicable Expertise to what you had before and shift your points to this or receive time reductions to work towards it as if you Retrained your old mastery.
**b**. You may accept Time Tokens to be used as in point 1a above.
**c**. You may apply for a Feat to somehow represent or emulate the old Mastery, if one does not already exist and can be worked out.
**d**. You may, to some degree, work with Staff to mix and match the above approaches.

Outside of these cases, ongoing queue timers will remain in place as they were before, with the exception that queued Rank 5 Masteries will have their existing queue timers reduced by 3 Months (84 Days) to account for the new timer length. Any existing queue timers will also be considered Cooldowns now instead of the old Cook Timers, meaning what was queued is immediately usable as described in the “Training Time” section above.

Note that Expertises, unlike in the old system, are not tied to having 50 ranks in a particular mechanical skill anymore unless they relate to certain specific skills (see Application, above) - instead, the requirement is simply to have at least 200 total SP on your character.

There are going to be niche cases and difficulties in the transition period from the old system to the new; this is unavoidable. Some characters will convert cleanly, others will not. We ask for patience as the transition is worked through.
