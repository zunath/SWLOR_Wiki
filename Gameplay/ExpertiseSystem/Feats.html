Feats

Feats, inspired by the same-named system in tabletop systems like Dungeons & Dragons, Pathfinder, and other d20-based systems, are ways to represent additional facets and capabilities your character has, without necessarily being things that make sense to be scaling levels of competency. The benefits Feats give are not standardized, and are subject to the rulings of whatever GM may be present and running any given event or interaction, but GMs are strongly encouraged to give them some kind of effect should they become relevant.

More so than Expertises, Feats are meant to be somewhat free-form. There will likely never be a full, comprehensive list of Feats, but there are a number that we have defined. Like with Expertises, new Feats can be proposed and applied for.

Feats are applied for and approved just like Masteries and Expertises.

Each player character starts with two (2) feats representing their journey through life up until the point of arriving in the game world, and it is recommended and expected that these beginning feats will fit the “Fluff” category mentioned above. Feat slots are unlocked at a rate of one for every 3 ranks of Expertise and once a character gains their Mastery (3☆), totaling to eight (8) at maximum. GMs may also rarely grant PC’s feats as rewards for events or plotlines, and bonus feats granted in this way do not count towards this cap — though this will be discussed amongst Staff first, and consideration will be taken for any existing GM-granted feats.

While the RP implication of most feats should be fairly clear, any ‘mechanical' benefit they provide within the various Dice systems used by GMs is somewhat up to interpretation by said GMs.

 

Fluff Feats

Fluff feats are meant to represent flavorful qualities or details about your character that may not necessarily be overly impactful or beneficial in events, but still provide context and roleplay. These are encouraged to be things like small quirks, hobbies, simple or non-heroic life skills, mundane professions, and other “background” details. Unlike most other Feats or Expertises, these can also be relatively broad in scope, or as specific as you like. 

Fluff Feats are also uniquely non-exclusive. That is to say, if one character has “Painter” as a Fluff feat, that doesn't mean that another character who does not have that feat cannot paint. Rather, Fluff feats imply a degree of thematic focus, meaning that it is an important enough facet of a character to be worth investing in by giving it one of these slots. It is highly encouraged that players “play up” their characters' Fluff feat when appropriate, and emphasize them in roleplay. They are here for flavor, like seasonings in a dish.

Generally, Fluff feats are not expected to give bonuses, but a GM or another player may well decide to take them into account if they do somehow apply to a given situation. For example, if a GM is running an event involving a ballroom dance and your character has a feat like “Dancer,” the GM may decide that your character can shine particularly well during those scenes and give you bonuses of some form. 

Fluff feats have no prerequisites and do not require application or approval; however, Staff may determine whether or not a feat qualifies as Fluff, or if it is impactful enough to warrant approval. Some qualities that are not suitable for Fluff Feats include things like sexuality, gender identity, and mental or physical disorders or disabilities.

Specialization Feats

Specialization Feats are tied directly to Expertises and represent a character having put focus into that specific field, allowing them greater capability toward those ends. Unlike Ability Feats, which might have a rank of a particular expertise as a prerequisite but represent a specific ability or skill, Specialization Feats are about expanding on a character's Expertises and what they can do with them. They are how a broad field is narrowed down.

Not all Expertises have specializations. Although it is common for players to request expertises (or masteries, in the past) with a particular focus, not all of these are fit to be Specializations. For example, if you wanted to take Expertise in Enhance Attribute with a particular focus on Agility or Speed, you are free to do so, but this is not worthy of a Specialization feat - you can simply voluntarily roleplay your character concentrating on that aspect of the expertise, perhaps appending a parenthetical note to represent this.

Often when an Expertise has Specializations available at its first Rank, players may choose one for free when they first put a point into said Expertise without consuming a Feat slot to do so. For example, a character putting a point into Medical expertise would be able to choose between the Field Medic or Doctor specializations, or a budding Pilot could choose to become a Starship Pilot or a Planetary Vehicle Pilot. In these cases, a character's Specialization within their Expertise will be reflected in the Expertise's name listed on their sheet in some clear way (e.g. Medical Doctor, Starship Pilot, Droidcraft Engineer). Some Specializations require a higher rank within their given Expertise, reflecting a greater degree of experience or skill to enter such a field.

A character may choose to take more than one Specialization within an Expertise, but any Specialization after the first (if the first was available for free at the beginning) requires taking that Specialization as a Feat.
